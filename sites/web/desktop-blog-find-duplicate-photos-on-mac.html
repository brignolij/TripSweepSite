How to find duplicate and similar photos on a Mac

Jeffrey Brignoli · November 10, 2026

Years of imports, camera cards and backups leave the same photos scattered across folders on a Mac. Some are exact copies; most are near-identical shots of the same moment. Here is how to find both, keep the best and free the space, without touching your Photos library.

What macOS offers

  • Finder: sorting a folder by name or size helps spot obvious copies such as “IMG_1234 copy.jpg”, but not two different shots of the same scene.
  • Photos: its Duplicates album only covers photos already in the Photos library, not your other folders or drives.

With PhotoCull, step by step

1. Install PhotoCull. Download it for free: macOS 11 or later on Apple Silicon, signed and notarized by Apple.

2. Open the folder. Click Open folder, or drop a folder onto the window. Subfolders are included, and so are external drives.

3. Look at the badges. PhotoCull compares the content of the photos, not their names: exact copies, bursts and near-identical shots get the same “≈ N” badge.

4. Keep the best one. Open a group to compare up to six photos side by side, keep the sharpest and mark the rest.

5. Delete safely. Marked files go to the Mac's Trash, where Put Back restores them if you change your mind.

Good to know

  • JPEG, HEIC, PNG, TIFF, WebP and RAW files (CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF, ORF, RW2, DNG) are supported, as well as MOV and MP4 videos.
  • Photos are dated from their EXIF data, so copied folders still sort by the day they were taken.
  • Sorting a folder is free and never limited. Everything runs on your Mac: no account, no upload.
  • Photos libraries and app bundles inside the folder are skipped, so PhotoCull never digs into another app's files.
