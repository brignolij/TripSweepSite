How to free up iPhone storage without paying for iCloud

Jeffrey Brignoli · November 3, 2026

Using an Android phone? Read the Android guide: How to free up Android storage without paying for Google One

“iPhone storage full” almost always means one thing: the Photos app. Photos and videos are usually the largest item in Settings → General → iPhone Storage, and most of that space is waste you can remove without losing a single memory.

1. Start with what weighs the most

Large videos are the fastest win: a few minutes of 4K video take as much room as hundreds of photos. Sort your videos by size, watch the first seconds, and delete the ones you will never open again. Screen recordings and videos sent to you by messaging apps are common offenders.

2. Delete similar photos, not just duplicates

The Photos app can find exact duplicates, but the real waste is near-identical shots: the burst of ten frames, five attempts at the same group photo, three selfies in a row. Keep the best of each group and delete the rest. This is where most people recover the most space, and the hardest part to do by hand.

3. Clear screenshots and blurry photos

Screenshots pile up unnoticed: tickets, maps, conversations you no longer need. Blurry photos and accidental shots are the same. They rarely deserve to stay, and they are quick to review as a batch.

4. Empty “Recently Deleted”

Deleted photos stay in the Recently Deleted album for 30 days and keep taking space. Once you are happy with your choices, empty it to actually get the storage back.

5. Clean up old trips, one by one

Old vacations are the biggest block of photos you never look at. Going trip by trip is easier than scrolling a library of thousands of photos: pick the heaviest trip, keep the highlights, delete the leftovers.

Do I need iCloud?

iCloud is a fine backup, but it is not a cleaning tool: it stores everything, including the duplicates. Cleaning first means you need less storage, wherever it is.
