How to sort your iPhone photos on a Mac

Jeffrey Brignoli · November 12, 2026

Using an Android phone? Read the Android guide: How to sort your Android photos on a Mac

Sorting a full camera roll on the iPhone means hours of tapping. On a Mac you see far more photos at once, select with the mouse and compare shots on a big screen. Here is how to do it, with the tools built into macOS and with a dedicated app.

With the tools built into macOS

  • Photos: imports a copy of your iPhone photos to the Mac. Useful as a backup, but it doesn't tell you what to delete on the iPhone.
  • Image Capture: lists the iPhone's photos as plain files, with no grouping by trip and no way to compare similar shots.

Both work, but with thousands of photos you are still scrolling one long list.

With PhotoCull, step by step

1. Install PhotoCull. Download it for free: macOS 11 or later on Apple Silicon, signed and notarized by Apple.

2. Plug in the iPhone. Connect it with a USB cable, unlock it and tap Trust.

3. Sort trip by trip. The Trips view groups your camera roll by date and place and shows the space each trip takes. Start with the heaviest.

4. Compare and mark. Similar photos carry a badge: open up to six side by side, keep the best and mark the rest, along with screenshots and big videos you don't need.

5. Delete safely. With iCloud Photos off and macOS 27 or later, PhotoCull deletes the photos directly over the USB cable. The deletion is permanent on the iPhone: the photos do not go to “Recently Deleted”. By default, PhotoCull keeps a copy of each deleted item on your Mac for 30 days, in a “PhotoCull Trash” folder in Pictures, so a mistake can be undone by importing the file again. If iCloud Photos is on, or your Mac runs macOS 26 or earlier, use the free TripSweep app instead: it deletes through “Recently Deleted”, so you have 30 days to change your mind.

Good to know

  • Everything stays on your Mac and your iPhone: no account, no cloud, no upload.
  • PhotoCull never modifies your originals and only deletes what you confirm.
  • From a Mac, direct deletion has no daily limit. Through TripSweep, free allows 6 deletions a day and Pro 15 (people who already bought Pro + Desktop keep unlimited). Browsing and sorting are free, and sorting a folder of photos on the Mac is never limited.
