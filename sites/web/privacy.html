Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 3, 2026

TripSweep ("the app") is developed by Jeffrey Brignoli. This page explains what data the app uses, where it goes, and why. The project's guiding principle is simple: your photos, videos and location never leave your iPhone or Android phone.

What the app does not do

  • It does not collect, send, or share your photos, videos, or metadata with anyone.
  • It does not create any user account and does not ask for any personal information (name, email...).
  • It does not contain any advertising SDK or cross-app tracking.
  • It does not rely on any server: place recognition (city/country) happens entirely offline, from data embedded in the app (GeoNames.org, CC BY 4.0 license), never querying an online service.

Access to your photo library

The app requests full access to your photo library (Photos) for two reasons:

  • Analyzing your photos and videos to group them by trip, calculate the space they take up, and find similar photos (near-duplicates) — this processing happens entirely on your device, using Apple's on-device frameworks (Photos and Vision).
  • Deleting the photos you explicitly choose to delete — deletion always goes through “Recently Deleted” (30 days), never an immediate permanent delete.

No photo, thumbnail, or metadata extracted from your photo library is ever transmitted off your device. The results of the analysis (your trips, and a compact numerical fingerprint of each analyzed photo used to compare them) are kept in a local database on your iPhone, excluded from backups, and removed when you delete the app.

Access to your location

The app requests access to your location only when you choose to manually add a “place” (for example “Home” or “Work”) in the places management feature. This location is stored locally on your device and never transmitted elsewhere. It is used only to compare distance with detected trips, so you can exclude them from the list if you wish.

Syncing with the Mac app

If you also use PhotoCull on your Mac, the companion app can exchange information with it via a direct local USB/Wi-Fi connection between your two Apple devices (a standard protocol used for iOS device syncing) — never over the Internet or via a third-party server.

In-app purchases

The app offers an optional paid feature (“Pro”, one-time purchase) handled exclusively by Apple via StoreKit. No payment information is collected, stored, or visible to the developer: Apple handles the entire transaction in accordance with its own privacy policy.

Analytics and diagnostics

The apps (TripSweep on iPhone and Android, PhotoCull on Mac and Windows) send anonymous usage statistics to PostHog, an analytics service hosted in the European Union, through a simple request with no third-party SDK. Each event contains only: the event name (for example “app opened” or “photos deleted”), counters (for example the number of photos deleted in one go and where they came from: viewer, trip or Mac), overall totals of your library once a day (number of photos, total size, share of similar photos, number of trips, share of photos that carry a location — only these totals, never the photos or places themselves), the app version, the operating-system version, the app language, and a random identifier generated on first launch. This identifier is not tied to any account, advertising identifier or person; it only lets us count how many devices are active per day, week or month, and it disappears when the app is uninstalled. Never sent: photos, videos, file names, locations, place or trip names, or any other content from your library. IP addresses are not stored with the events. You can turn this off at any time (TripSweep: About tab > Anonymous usage statistics; PhotoCull: language menu > Anonymous usage statistics). Apart from that, the apps do not integrate any advertising or third-party tracking tool. If you have enabled analytics data sharing with app developers (iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements), Apple may send anonymized crash reports — this feature is handled entirely by Apple, independently of the app. This website (not the apps) uses Google Analytics 4 in a cookieless configuration to count visits: no cookie or local identifier is stored in your browser, advertising features are disabled, and no photo, library, or location data from the apps is involved.

Your rights

Since no personal data is collected or stored by the developer outside your device, there is no external database to consult, correct, or delete. You retain full control at all times over the permissions granted to the app via iOS Settings.

Android version

The Android version of TripSweep follows exactly the same principle: your photos, videos and location never leave your phone. It requests access to your photos and videos (on Android 14 and later you can grant partial access), and to the location stored in your photos' metadata (“media location” permission) to recognize where each trip took place; this information is processed only on your device, in a local database that is excluded from backups and removed when you uninstall the app. Similar-photo detection uses on-device models (including Google ML Kit for faces), with no network connection. Your current location is requested only when you choose to add a “place” manually. Photos you delete go to the Android system trash (30 days). The optional “Pro” purchase is handled exclusively by Google Play: no payment information is visible to the developer. The app contains no advertising SDK and sends only the anonymous usage statistics described above, which you can turn off in the About tab.
