How to sort a big folder of photos, or a whole external drive

Jeffrey Brignoli · November 10, 2026

Everyone has one: a folder called “Photos to sort”, an old phone backup, an external drive filled over ten years. Thousands of files, no order, many near-duplicates. Here is a method to get through it in one sitting.

1. Open the whole folder at once

Don't go folder by folder. Open the top folder in PhotoCull (Open folder, or drop it onto the window): subfolders are included, and each photo is dated from its EXIF data rather than from the day it was copied.

2. Let the trips appear

The Trips view groups photos by date and place and shows the space each group takes. Ten years of files become a list of trips and events, and you start with the heaviest.

3. Collapse the near-duplicates

Bursts, repeated takes and copies of the same shot carry a “≈ N” badge. Compare up to six side by side, keep the best one, mark the rest. This step alone often removes a large share of the files.

4. Mark the obvious leftovers

Screenshots, blurry shots, accidental videos: mark them as you go. Nothing is deleted until you confirm.

5. Delete safely

Confirmed files go to the Trash on a Mac or the Recycle Bin on Windows, so a mistake can be undone. Empty it once you are sure.

Good to know

  • RAW files from cameras (CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF, ORF, RW2, DNG) are supported alongside JPEG, HEIC, PNG and videos.
  • Sorting folders is free and never limited, on Mac (download) and Windows (download).
  • Your originals are never modified: the only change PhotoCull makes is the deletion you confirm.
