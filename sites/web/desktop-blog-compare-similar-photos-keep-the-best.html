How to compare similar photos and keep the best one

You took six photos of the same moment and they all look fine. Choosing the best takes seconds with the right method, and a few minutes with the wrong one.

What to check, in this order

  1. Focus. Zoom on the eyes or the main subject. A blurry subject is disqualifying.
  2. Expressions. Eyes open, natural smiles, nobody mid-blink.
  3. Framing. Nothing cut off, a clean background, the horizon straight.
  4. Moment. If two shots are equal, keep the one that feels more alive.

Why side by side matters

Flipping between photos makes small differences hard to see. Seeing them next to each other at the same size makes the sharpest or best-framed one obvious. A computer screen shows up to six at once, where a phone shows one.

A faster routine

Group similar photos first, so you never hunt for them in the library. For each group, open the comparison, pick one and mark the others. You decide in seconds, not minutes.

Keep, don't regret

When you are unsure, keep two. Deletions on an iPhone go through “Recently Deleted” for 30 days, so you can still change your mind.
